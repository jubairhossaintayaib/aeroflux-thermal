Air Conditioning Repair

Air conditioning repairs that bring comfort back.

Uneven cooling, unfamiliar noises or a system that will not start? Get a clear route from diagnosis to repair for your home or workplace.

Find the fault. Restore the comfort.

An air conditioner should quietly get on with keeping your rooms comfortable. When it starts dripping, struggles to reach the set temperature or stops responding, the cause could sit anywhere from a blocked filter to a control or refrigerant fault.

A structured assessment helps avoid unnecessary replacements. Tell us the make, model, any error code and when the problem happens. The proposed visit can then focus on the symptoms, safe access and the parts that may be needed.

When to arrange a visit

  • Warm air or poor airflow from the indoor unit
  • Water dripping indoors or a blocked condensate drain
  • Unusual rattling, vibration or repeated stopping
  • A fault code, unresponsive controls or a unit that will not start

What a visit can include

  1. Inspect the indoor and outdoor units and discuss the fault history.
  2. Check accessible filters, airflow, drainage, controls and electrical operation.
  3. Explain the findings and set out a repair quotation before additional work.
  4. Test the system after the agreed repair and explain any follow-up care.

Your repair options, clearly explained

Some problems can be resolved with servicing or an accessible component replacement. Others need specialist refrigerant work, a return visit or a part ordered for your model. The engineer should explain these possibilities before you authorise the next step.

Refrigerant work must be completed by people with the appropriate qualifications and certification. Never open the refrigerant circuit yourself.

Ready to take the next step?

Have your postcode, equipment make and model, and a brief description of the issue ready. If possible, include the fault code and explain any access requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Who should install or repair my air conditioning?

Use an appropriately qualified contractor. Work involving fluorinated refrigerants requires the relevant F-gas qualifications and, where applicable, company certification. Ask for the credentials relevant to your equipment before agreeing to the work.

GOV.UK F-gas guidance

Should I repair my existing system or replace it?

Consider the diagnosis, repair price, parts availability, breakdown history and running costs together. A single fault does not automatically mean a replacement is necessary. Request a written comparison of the practical options before deciding.

Can I get a price before any work starts?

A useful quotation should separate the visit or diagnostic charge, recommended work, parts and applicable VAT. Some faults need an on-site inspection first. Ask what is included, which options are available and how additional work would be agreed.
