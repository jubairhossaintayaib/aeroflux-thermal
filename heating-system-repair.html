Heating System Repair

A warmer home starts with the right repair.

Practical support for boiler faults, cold radiators, unreliable controls and heat-pump problems across the Glasgow area.

Get to the cause of your heating problem.

A cold room does not always mean the boiler has failed. Heating performance depends on the appliance, circulation, controls and the way the whole system works together. A useful diagnosis looks beyond the most obvious symptom.

Share the equipment type, any displayed fault code, and whether you have lost heating, hot water or both. That information helps establish the right kind of appointment and the qualifications needed for the job.

When to arrange a visit

  • No heating, no hot water or intermittent operation
  • Radiators that stay cold or heat unevenly
  • Repeated pressure loss or an unfamiliar appliance noise
  • Thermostats and heating schedules that do not respond

What a visit can include

  1. Review the symptoms, controls and recent service history.
  2. Assess the appliance and connected system within the engineer’s qualifications.
  3. Explain the repair options, parts requirements and likely disruption.
  4. Check operation after the agreed work and give practical handover advice.

Repair advice that fits your home

The right outcome might be a control adjustment, replacement component or further system investigation. If repair is no longer practical, compare a replacement against the existing system’s condition and your household needs. A written scope should explain the basis of the recommendation.

For gas appliance work, use a Gas Safe registered engineer qualified for that appliance. If you smell gas, call 0800 111 999 in Great Britain rather than using this website.

Ready to take the next step?

Have your postcode, equipment make and model, and a brief description of the issue ready. If possible, include the fault code and explain any access requirements.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I have my boiler or HVAC system serviced?

Arrange an annual service for gas appliances with a suitably qualified Gas Safe registered engineer. Heat pumps and air conditioning should follow the manufacturer’s service schedule; usage and the building can affect the interval. A routine check helps spot wear before a breakdown.

HSE gas safety guidance

Should I repair my existing system or replace it?

Consider the diagnosis, repair price, parts availability, breakdown history and running costs together. A single fault does not automatically mean a replacement is necessary. Request a written comparison of the practical options before deciding.

What should I do if I smell gas?

Call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 in Great Britain. Do not use an online enquiry form for a suspected gas leak. Open doors and windows if safe, and follow the emergency service’s instructions.
