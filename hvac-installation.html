HVAC Installation

A better climate, designed around you.

From a single comfortable room to a complete property upgrade, start with a survey that considers how you actually use your space.

Good design comes before new equipment.

A successful installation starts with the building. Room use, insulation, glazing, outdoor space and the existing heating system all affect the right solution. The survey should turn those details into a practical specification.

Explore air conditioning, heat pumps and wider heating or ventilation improvements with a clear understanding of what each system can do. An air-to-air system that heats and cools rooms is different from an air-to-water heat pump supplying radiators and hot water.

When to arrange a visit

  • An ageing or unreliable heating and cooling system
  • Rooms that overheat in summer or stay cold in winter
  • A renovation, extension or change in how a building is used
  • A planned move towards a lower-carbon heating system

What a visit can include

  1. Survey the property and establish heating or cooling requirements.
  2. Agree equipment locations, access, controls and the installation scope.
  3. Check necessary permissions, electrical work and specialist requirements.
  4. Install, test and commission the agreed system, followed by a clear handover.

Know what happens before installation day

Your quotation should identify the equipment, supporting works, exclusions and expected timetable. Ask about maintenance access, noise considerations and product warranty conditions. For a heat pump, confirm whether radiators, pipework or hot-water storage need attention as part of the design.

Funding and planning requirements depend on your property and location. Check eligibility and required approvals before work starts; a grant or saving is never guaranteed by this preview.

Ready to take the next step?

Have your postcode, equipment make and model, and a brief description of the issue ready. If possible, include the fault code and explain any access requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Will a heat pump keep my home warm in a Scottish winter?

A correctly designed heat pump can provide heating in cold weather. The important starting points are the property’s heat loss, insulation, radiator or underfloor-heating output and suitable controls. Have the whole system assessed rather than choosing equipment on floor area alone.

Energy Saving Trust

Do I need planning permission for a heat pump in Glasgow?

Some domestic heat-pump installations in Scotland can use permitted development rights, subject to conditions. Cooling equipment, flats, listed buildings and conservation areas may need different checks. Confirm your proposal with the local planning authority before work begins.

Scottish Government planning guidance

Is there help towards the cost of a heat pump in Scotland?

Home Energy Scotland offers guidance on grants and loans for eligible home improvements. Eligibility, installer requirements and funding availability matter, so obtain advice and the required approval before committing to work. This website does not offer or approve finance.

Home Energy Scotland funding

How long does a heating or cooling installation take?

Timing depends on the equipment, access, pipework, electrical supply and any changes to radiators or hot-water storage. A survey should establish the programme, expected interruption to heating and when testing and handover will happen.
