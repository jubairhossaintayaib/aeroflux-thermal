Emergency HVAC Service

Support when your comfort cannot wait.

A sudden breakdown can disrupt the whole day. Start with safe triage and clear information about the next available help.

First, understand what has stopped working.

Losing heat, cooling or hot water can feel urgent, especially when vulnerable people or a business depend on the system. Describe what has failed, when it happened and whether there are any signs of an immediate hazard.

For an ordinary equipment breakdown, useful information includes your postcode, the appliance type, its error code and safe access arrangements. A response window and call-out charge should be confirmed before an engineer is dispatched.

When to arrange a visit

  • Complete loss of heating or hot water
  • A cooling failure affecting occupied business premises
  • Equipment repeatedly shutting down or showing a fault
  • A system leak or malfunction requiring prompt assessment

What a visit can include

  1. Identify whether the issue belongs with an emergency utility service.
  2. Gather fault details and assess the appropriate engineer and next steps.
  3. Confirm availability, any diagnostic charge and access arrangements.
  4. Explain repair options, parts availability and any required return visit.

Clear expectations in a stressful moment

An emergency appointment does not always mean an immediate permanent repair. Safe isolation, a temporary measure or ordering a model-specific part may be necessary. You should receive a clear explanation of what can be done at the visit and what happens next.

Suspected gas leak? Call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 in Great Britain. For immediate danger to life, call 999. This demo is not monitored and cannot dispatch an engineer.

Ready to take the next step?

Have your postcode, equipment make and model, and a brief description of the issue ready. If possible, include the fault code and explain any access requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I smell gas?

Call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 in Great Britain. Do not use an online enquiry form for a suspected gas leak. Open doors and windows if safe, and follow the emergency service’s instructions.

HSE emergency guidance

Should I repair my existing system or replace it?

Consider the diagnosis, repair price, parts availability, breakdown history and running costs together. A single fault does not automatically mean a replacement is necessary. Request a written comparison of the practical options before deciding.

Can I get a price before any work starts?

A useful quotation should separate the visit or diagnostic charge, recommended work, parts and applicable VAT. Some faults need an on-site inspection first. Ask what is included, which options are available and how additional work would be agreed.
