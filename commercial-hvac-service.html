Commercial HVAC Service

Comfort that keeps your business moving.

Heating, cooling and ventilation support shaped around the needs of offices, shops, hospitality venues and managed properties.

Plan around people, premises and business hours.

Indoor comfort affects staff, visitors and the way a business operates. Commercial equipment also needs accessible servicing, accurate records and a clear plan for faults that could interrupt work.

Start with an equipment overview and site survey. Understanding occupied hours, critical areas, access restrictions and existing service arrangements helps shape a sensible maintenance or repair proposal.

When to arrange a visit

  • Inconsistent temperatures across offices or customer areas
  • Repeated equipment faults and unplanned interruptions
  • Poor airflow, noise or concerns about ventilation performance
  • An upcoming refurbishment, tenancy change or system replacement

What a visit can include

  1. Review the equipment schedule and the building’s operating requirements.
  2. Assess condition, maintenance access and reported performance issues.
  3. Propose repairs or a planned maintenance scope around business hours.
  4. Record completed work and identify priorities for future attention.

A clear scope for ongoing care

A commercial maintenance proposal should identify covered assets, visit frequency, reporting and any response commitment. Explain which areas are business-critical and request a separate price for out-of-hours work where needed. Parts, access equipment and replacement work should be clearly included or excluded.

Service obligations and refrigerant checks depend on the equipment and refrigerant type. The appropriate specialist should confirm what applies to your building.

Ready to take the next step?

Have your postcode, equipment make and model, and a brief description of the issue ready. If possible, include the fault code and explain any access requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Who should install or repair my air conditioning?

Use an appropriately qualified contractor. Work involving fluorinated refrigerants requires the relevant F-gas qualifications and, where applicable, company certification. Ask for the credentials relevant to your equipment before agreeing to the work.

GOV.UK F-gas guidance

How long does a heating or cooling installation take?

Timing depends on the equipment, access, pipework, electrical supply and any changes to radiators or hot-water storage. A survey should establish the programme, expected interruption to heating and when testing and handover will happen.

Home Energy Scotland installation guide

Can I get a price before any work starts?

A useful quotation should separate the visit or diagnostic charge, recommended work, parts and applicable VAT. Some faults need an on-site inspection first. Ask what is included, which options are available and how additional work would be agreed.
